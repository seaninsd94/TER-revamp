Planner Prospecting System

A structured framework to identify, qualify, rank, and approach Southern California planners who coordinate $100K+ events

1. Target Market Geography

Focus on high-density luxury pockets. You don't want all of Southern California.

Primary Markets

  • San Diego — Rancho Santa Fe, La Jolla estates
  • Newport Beach — Coastal estates, yacht-adjacent
  • Los Angeles — Private estates, Hollywood Hills
  • Santa Barbara — Montecito estates

Secondary Expansion

  • Palm Springs — Luxury desert buyouts
  • Malibu — Private oceanfront properties

Event Signals You Want

  • Estate properties (not hotel ballrooms)
  • Tented builds
  • 175–300+ guests
  • Full production lighting
  • Multi-day events

3. How Many to Source

Tier Count Description
Tier 1 20 High-production luxury planners
Tier 2 20 Solid high-end, scaling planners
Aspirational 10 Stretch targets / emerging luxury
TOTAL 50 You do not need more.

4. How to Identify Them

Method 1 — Venue Tag Mining

Search Instagram for events at Rancho Santa Fe estates, La Jolla venues, Montecito private estates, Malibu properties. Click event photos. Identify planners tagged. Add to sheet.

Method 2 — Publication Backtracking

Search: "Southern California wedding featured in Brides" → Open article → Scroll to vendor credits → Extract planner. This filters out low-budget planners instantly.

Method 3 — Production Vendor Reverse Engineering

Look at major tent/production companies in SoCal. Scroll through their tagged posts. See which planners consistently appear on large builds. Add those planners.

5. Prioritization Model

Once your 50 are listed, segment by approach strategy:

TIER A — Top 10

Estate events • $200K+ aesthetic • Multi-day builds • Clear production sophistication

  • Personalized email
  • Custom pitch deck
  • Possibly mailed lookbook

TIER B — Next 20

Solid high-end weddings • Strong aesthetic • Some tent builds

  • Standard intro email
  • Follow-up sequence
  • Light relationship building

TIER C — Remaining 20

Emerging luxury planners • Good potential • Still scaling

  • Soft introduction
  • Social engagement first

6. What You're Looking For

Planners who align with your narrative approach:

  • Care about storytelling
  • Use words like "immersive," "intentional," "personal"
  • Show custom installations
  • Avoid Pinterest-heavy duplication

You want alignment, not volume.

7. What to Avoid

Red Flags — Skip These Planners

  • Market primarily on affordability
  • Offer partial planning only
  • Show primarily venue-based weddings (no builds)
  • Have no evidence of production scale

They won't convert into $15K+ environment projects.
