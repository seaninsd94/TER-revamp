Power & Infrastructure

Complete electrical infrastructure is essential for tent-based events at private estates, vineyards, and venues without adequate power service. This guide covers equipment requirements, load calculations, and safety considerations.

Power Distribution Components

Component Purpose When Required
Generator Primary power source for entire event infrastructure Any venue without adequate electrical service (most private estates, vineyards, open fields)
Spider Box / Distro Box Splits generator power into multiple protected circuits with breakers All events requiring distributed power for lighting, HVAC, catering, entertainment
Quad Boxes 4-outlet power stations placed throughout tent footprint Position every 20-30 feet for vendor access; catering stations, DJ booth, photo areas
Commercial Extension Cords Heavy-duty 10-12 gauge cables, 50-100ft runs Connect distribution system to outlet stations; sized for load requirements
Cable Ramps / Cord Covers Protect cords and prevent trip hazards in walkways Any cord crossing guest pathways, entrance areas, dance floor perimeter
Surge Protectors Protect sensitive equipment from power fluctuations AV equipment, lighting controllers, catering refrigeration, DJ/band equipment

Load Calculation Reference

Use this reference to calculate total power requirements based on equipment in use:

Climate Control

  • Portable AC unit (5-ton): 30-40 amps
  • Portable AC unit (3-ton): 20-25 amps
  • Patio heater (electric): 15-20 amps
  • Industrial fan: 2-5 amps

Lighting

  • Chandelier cluster: 5-10 amps
  • Uplighting (per zone): 3-5 amps
  • String lights (100ft): 2-3 amps
  • Pin spots / accent: 1-2 amps

Catering Equipment

  • Refrigeration unit: 10-15 amps
  • Coffee service: 15-20 amps
  • Chafing dish warmers: 5-10 amps
  • Ice machine: 10-15 amps

Entertainment

  • DJ setup (full): 20-30 amps
  • Live band: 30-50 amps
  • Photo booth: 10-15 amps
  • Projection/screens: 5-10 amps

Example: 200 Guest Grand Celebration

Climate (2 × 5-ton AC)70 amps
Lighting (chandeliers + uplighting)25 amps
Catering (refrigeration + coffee + warmers)35 amps
DJ/Entertainment25 amps
Miscellaneous (charging, photo booth)15 amps
Subtotal170 amps
+ 20% Buffer34 amps
Total Required204 amps

Recommendation: 60-80 kW generator with 200A distribution

Safety & Compliance

  • GFCI Protection: All outdoor outlets must be GFCI-protected
  • Grounding: Generators must be properly grounded per local code
  • Clearance: Generators positioned minimum 20ft from tent, downwind
  • Fuel: Adequate fuel for runtime + 25% reserve; refueling protocol
  • Noise: Consider generator placement relative to ceremony/reception areas
  • Backup: For critical events, secondary generator on standby

Flooring & Surface Systems

Professional event flooring transforms uneven outdoor terrain into elegant, safe surfaces. This guide covers subflooring systems, finished flooring options, and coverage calculations.

Subflooring Systems

Subflooring creates a stable, level base and is required when installing over grass, uneven terrain, or specialty surfaces.

System Type Best Applications Technical Notes
Plywood Subflooring Grass lawns, standard outdoor venues, garden settings 3/4" CDX plywood on 2×4 frame; most cost-effective option; protects turf from damage
Leveling Subfloor System Sloped terrain, hillside venues, uneven ground Adjustable leg system accommodates up to 18" grade change; engineered for stability
Pool Cover + Subfloor Events over swimming pools Requires engineering certification; weight-rated for occupancy; custom fabrication
Interlocking Panel System Large-scale events, quick installation Modular plastic or aluminum panels; tool-free assembly; reusable
Tent Flooring Frame Integrated with tent structure Perimeter frame ties into tent anchoring; provides clean edge transition

Finished Flooring Options

Flooring Type Aesthetic Best Applications Considerations
White Vinyl Clean, modern, bright Cocktail areas, modern weddings, corporate events Shows scuffs; requires touch-up during event
Black/White Checkered Classic, formal, dramatic Dance floors, grand entrances, gala events Iconic look; photographs well

Transition & Safety Elements

Element Purpose & Requirements
ADA Ramps Wheelchair accessibility at all entry points; 1:12 slope ratio maximum; 36" minimum width; handrails for rises over 6"
Transition Strips Smooth edge treatment between different flooring surfaces; beveled aluminum or rubber; prevents trip hazards
Entrance Mats Capture dirt and moisture at entry points; logo/custom branding available; scraper + wiper combination for outdoor
Aisle Runners Define ceremonial pathways; protect flooring; available in custom colors and widths
Non-Slip Coating Required for dance floors; applied to all surfaces in wet weather conditions; clear matte finish
Edge Trim Finished perimeter treatment; hides subfloor edge; available in matching or contrasting colors

Coverage Calculations

Space Allocation Guidelines

Calculate square footage based on intended use and guest count:

Per-Guest Allocations

  • Dining (seated): 12-15 sq ft/guest
  • Cocktail (standing): 8-10 sq ft/guest
  • Dance floor: 4-5 sq ft/guest
    (assume 40-50% of guests dancing)
  • Ceremony seating: 8-10 sq ft/guest
  • Lounge areas: 15-20 sq ft/guest

Fixed Allocations

  • Lounge vignette: 100-150 sq ft each
  • Bar station: 100-120 sq ft each
  • Buffet line: 100-150 sq ft
  • DJ/Band area: 150-300 sq ft
  • Entry/transitions: 150-250 sq ft
  • Catering staging: 200-400 sq ft
