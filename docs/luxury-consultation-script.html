Luxury Consultation Script

A structured approach to pre-qualify and elevate client conversations

PURPOSE OF THIS SCRIPT

Guide discovery conversations that reveal client stories, establish value, and position our narrative approach before discussing logistics or pricing.

I
Framing
Set the tone before logistics

"Before we discuss logistics, I'd love to understand what this celebration represents for you."

This opener immediately signals that we are different. We lead with meaning, not inventory. Let them share openly before asking structured questions.

II
Memory Extraction
Uncover the story beneath the event

Ask these questions with genuine curiosity. Take notes. These details become the foundation of their environment design.

  • What season or location defines your relationship?
    Listen for: first trips, meaningful places, weather/atmosphere associations
  • Is there an object that holds emotional weight?
    Listen for: heirlooms, photographs, letters, textiles, keepsakes
  • What feeling do you want guests to experience upon arrival?
    Listen for: emotional words (warmth, surprise, intimacy, grandeur)
  • What part of your journey deserves honoring?
    Listen for: transitions, achievements, relationships, losses, growth
Key Actions: Listen actively. Take detailed notes. Repeat back themes you hear to confirm understanding and show you're genuinely engaged.
III
Reframing Into Environment
Translate their story into design language

This is where you demonstrate your expertise. Connect their memories to tangible design elements.

"So what I'm hearing is warmth, legacy, and transition. We would translate that into layered textures, generational details, and progressive lighting shifts throughout the evening."

Customize this based on what they shared. Examples of translations:

They say: "Coastal, peaceful"
You say: "Cool-toned lighting, natural textures, flowing layouts"
They say: "Family traditions"
You say: "Heritage color palettes, heirloom integration, generational details"
They say: "Our journey together"
You say: "Progressive spatial transitions, story arc through the evening"
They say: "Intimate, meaningful"
You say: "Warm lighting, conversation-focused layouts, tactile materials"
Now they see your value. You've transformed abstract feelings into concrete design direction. This is what separates us from rental catalogs.
IV
Investment Framing
Position pricing within value context

Only discuss investment after you've established value through the story conversation.

"Our narrative environments typically begin in the mid-five-figure range depending on scope, production, and installation complexity. We design everything bespoke around your story."

Pause.

Let them respond. Give them space to process. Silence is powerful here.

If they hesitate on price: "I understand. Our approach isn't for everyone, and that's okay. For clients who value deeply personal environments, this investment reflects the research, design, sourcing, and execution that goes into translating a story into a space."
V
Close
Establish clear next steps

"If this direction feels aligned, the next step is a formal design proposal. We'll craft a preliminary narrative concept and investment outline for your review."

What happens next:

Schedule follow-up within 48 hours to send proposal
Send thank-you email within 24 hours referencing specific details from conversation
Prepare preliminary narrative concept based on their story
Include 2-3 visual references that align with their emotional themes

Key Principles to Remember

  • Lead with story, not inventory. Never open with "what do you need?"
  • Listen more than you speak. They should talk 70% of the time in Phase II.
  • Use their words. Repeat back their language when reframing into design.
  • Don't rush to price. Value must be established first.
  • Be comfortable with silence. Let them process.
  • Not every client is right for us. Qualify gracefully.
